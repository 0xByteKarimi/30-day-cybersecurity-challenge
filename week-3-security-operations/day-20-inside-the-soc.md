## 📚 Learned

I completed the **Security Operations and the Defense Analyst** course.

SOC stands for **Security Operations Center**. It is the team and place where security analysts monitor, detect, and respond to threats 24/7.

What I learned:
- **Tier 1 (Triage)** — The first line. They look at alerts and decide if they are real.
- **Tier 2 (Investigation)** — They dig deeper into confirmed alerts.
- **Tier 3 (Threat Hunting)** — They look for threats that automated tools missed.
- **SOC Manager** — Runs the team and reports to leadership.
- **Shift work** — SOCs run 24/7, so analysts work in shifts.

🔗 [Security Operations and the Defense Analyst](https://u.cisco.com)

---

## ✅ What I Did

I learned how a typical day in a SOC looks.

### A day in the life of a SOC analyst

1. **Start of shift:** Review open tickets and handover notes from the previous shift.
2. **Monitor alerts:** Watch the SIEM dashboard for new alerts.
3. **Triage:** Decide which alerts are real and which are false positives.
4. **Investigate:** Dig into real alerts. Check logs, endpoints, and network traffic.
5. **Escalate:** If it is serious, pass it to Tier 2 or Tier 3.
6. **Document:** Write clear notes so the next analyst understands what happened.
7. **End of shift:** Hand over open items to the next shift.

### The main tools they use
- SIEM (like Splunk)
- EDR (endpoint detection and response)
- Ticketing systems (like Jira or ServiceNow)
- Threat intelligence platforms

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- The SOC is the front line of defense.
- Most SOC work is triage. Deciding what is real and what is noise.
- Clear documentation is as important as technical skill.
- Teamwork matters. Analysts rely on each other across shifts.
- Threat hunting is proactive. It is not waiting for alerts.
