## 📚 Learned

I learned how passwords get stolen and how **multi-factor authentication (MFA)** stops most account takeovers.

MFA adds a second proof of identity:
- Something you **know** (password)
- Something you **have** (phone, key)
- Something you **are** (fingerprint, face)

🔗 [What is multi-factor authentication (MFA)?](https://www.cisco.com)

---

## ✅ What I Did

### Enabled MFA on three critical accounts
1. **Email account** — enabled app-based MFA
2. **Banking account** — enabled SMS + app MFA
3. **Cloud storage account** — enabled app-based MFA

### Switched one weak password to a passphrase
**Before:** A short password with a number at the end. Easy to guess.
**After:** A passphrase made of four random words. Longer, easier to remember, much harder to crack.

---

## 🔑 Why Passphrases Work

- Length matters more than complexity.
- `Tr0ub4dor&3` is short and hard to remember.
- `correct-horse-battery-staple` is long, easy to remember, and takes centuries to brute-force.
- Never reuse a passphrase across accounts.

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- MFA blocks most credential-based attacks, even if the password leaks.
- Passphrases beat short, complex passwords.
- Password managers help — one strong passphrase unlocks the rest.
- SMS MFA is better than nothing, but app-based MFA is stronger.
